📌 How to Use This Practice Pack
▸ Part 1 — Example Match  (~15 min)

Read the opinion statement. Choose the ONE example that most directly and specifically supports it. Wrong options are designed to mislead: they may be true, relevant, or sound convincing — but they do NOT prove the opinion as directly as the correct answer. After each question, read the TIP to understand what makes the winning example work.
▸ Part 2 — 5W1H Detail Builder

A strong spoken example always answers: WHO did this? WHEN? WHERE? WHY? WHAT happened? HOW did they do it? Each exercise gives you a sentence framework with six blanks. Drag the correct phrase from the Word Bank into each slot. Read the completed example aloud to feel how the details create a vivid, believable story.
▸ Part 3 — Best Example Selector

Twenty sentences are provided, but only four of them belong in the best version of the example. Your job: identify which four, then arrange them in the correct order (1 = first sentence, 4 = last). Look for sentences that are: SPECIFIC (names, numbers, places), RELEVANT (directly prove the opinion), SEQUENTIAL (tell a clear story), and VARIED (not repetitive).

Topic: Hobbies
Q1.  Opinion:  
"People who have hobbies tend to be more emotionally balanced because regular creative activities provide a healthy outlet for their frustrations."
Which example BEST supports this opinion?
(A)  My friend Lisa, who used to get easily frustrated at work, started taking weekly pottery classes last year; she now spends every Saturday afternoon shaping clay at a studio downtown, and her colleagues have noticed that she handles workplace pressure much more calmly.
(B)  Many people around the world enjoy collecting stamps, coins, or vintage items as a way to pass time during weekends.
(C)  Research shows that adults who pursue hobbies report higher levels of job satisfaction compared to those who do not.
(D)  Some hobbies, such as rock climbing or surfing, can actually be quite dangerous if practitioners do not receive proper training and equipment.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: Notice how Option A directly maps frustration → creative activity → emotional change with a real person, time, place, and observable result.
