Part 1   Read Choice
阅读题目要求，选出**最扣题、最适合作为独立题论点句**的选项。

Q1. Prompt:
Some schools require students to wear uniforms. Do you agree or disagree? Which thesis BEST supports **agreeing** with school uniforms?
(A) Uniforms reduce visible economic inequality and help students focus on learning rather than morning fashion choices, which strengthens the school community.
(B) Teenagers need creative freedom, and uniforms remove all opportunities for self-expression.
(C) Many schools in different countries have different rules, so policies vary.
(D) Uniforms are usually cheaper than buying many casual outfits.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: Strong agree thesis names concrete benefits aligned with the question (equality, focus); (B) is disagree; (C) avoids a stance; (D) is a minor point without the main educational argument.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q2. Prompt:
Do you prefer to study alone or in a group? Which thesis BEST supports **preferring group study**?
(A) Group study forces passive students to participate because other members ask questions that expose gaps in understanding.
(B) Studying alone is quieter, so it is always more efficient for everyone.
(C) Libraries are good places to study because they have many books.
(D) Some people dislike social interaction and should never join groups.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: The thesis must defend **group** study; (A) gives a mechanism (peer questioning); the others argue alone, change topic, or are overly absolute against groups.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q3. Prompt:
Your city has extra funds. Should it build more parks or improve public transportation? Which thesis BEST supports **choosing parks**?
(A) Accessible green space improves mental health and encourages physical activity for residents of all ages, especially those who cannot afford gym memberships.
(B) Public transportation reduces traffic, so cities should never fund parks.
(C) Both parks and transport are important, so the city should split the budget evenly without prioritizing either goal.
(D) Parks are beautiful, and beauty is everything a city needs.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: (A) ties parks to concrete public-health outcomes; (B) contradicts the chosen side; (C) refuses to pick (bad for forced-choice prompts); (D) is vague hype without reasoning depth.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q4. Prompt:
Do you agree that **social media does more harm than good** for teenagers? Which thesis BEST supports the **harm** position?
(A) Constant exposure to filtered success stories triggers comparison cycles that predict higher anxiety and lower self-esteem in longitudinal studies of adolescent users.
(B) Social media helps teenagers stay connected, so it is mostly positive.
(C) Teenagers use many kinds of apps on their phones for different purposes.
(D) Parents should buy phones for their children at younger ages.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: (A) names a **mechanism + population** tied to harm; (B) supports the opposite side; (C)(D) do not advance the harm claim.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q5. Prompt:
Should universities require a public speaking course for all majors? Which thesis BEST supports **yes**?
(A) Regardless of field, graduates must present ideas in meetings and interviews; structured speaking practice lowers anxiety and improves clarity under time pressure.
(B) Public speaking is scary, so universities should eliminate all presentation requirements.
(C) Engineering students need math more than speeches, so only arts majors should speak.
(D) Some famous leaders were poor public speakers, so training does not matter.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: (A) links **all majors** to real workplace demand; others are irrelevant, opposite, or use weak celebrity exceptions.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q6. Prompt:
Is it better to pursue one career path early or explore several fields first? Which thesis BEST supports **exploring several fields first**?
(A) Early exploration reveals mismatch before sunk costs grow, because transferring after two years in the wrong track wastes less tuition and morale than switching after a decade.
(B) People should choose one job at age eighteen and never reconsider.
(C) Salary is the only metric that matters when choosing work.
(D) Exploring is fun, and fun is the only goal of education.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: (A) offers **trade-off reasoning** (cost of early lock-in); (B)(C) attack exploration; (D) is shallow.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q7. Prompt:
Should governments ban single-use plastics in restaurants? Which thesis BEST supports **banning**?
(A) Visible plastic waste enters waterways quickly, harms wildlife, and burdens municipal cleanup budgets; reusables shift costs to producers and cut litter at the source.
(B) Plastic is cheap, so restaurants will always prefer it.
(C) People enjoy takeout food, therefore environmental policy is impossible.
(D) Banning plastics will instantly solve every environmental problem on Earth.
Answer: A
💡 TIP: (A) connects ban to **mechanism + public cost**; (B)(C) are defeatist non-arguments; (D) overclaims and is easy to attack.
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q8. Prompt:
Which option is the **clearest off-topic thesis** for: "Should students be allowed to use AI tools when drafting essays?"
(A) AI tools can reduce brainstorming time and help struggling students outline ideas faster if instructors require disclosure and a human final revision step.
(B) University dining halls should offer more vegan meals because plant-based diets reduce carbon emissions.
(C) Transparent AI use with instructor guidelines can teach editing skills while preventing undisclosed ghostwriting.
(D) Without rules, AI encourages plagiarism; therefore schools must pair access with explicit integrity standards.
Answer: B
💡 TIP: (B) shifts entirely to **cafeteria menus**—classic off-topic; others engage AI, drafting, or academic integrity.
